This specification describes a data model with which one or more recognized entities, such as one or more persons and/or organizations, can be described as known to perform specific actions, such as issuing or verifying a [=verifiable credential=]. The data model enables the publication or direct sharing of such information, providing a cryptographically-verifiable and privacy-preserving mechanism through which a [=holder=] can demonstrate that an entity whose credential they are using is recognized within a particular ecosystem. The specification is designed to interoperate with existing "trust infrastructures", such as X.509 certificate authority lists and ETSI Trust Service Lists, while enabling new decentralized ecosystems to be built using [=verifiable credentials=].

This is an experimental specification and is undergoing regular revisions. It is not fit for production deployment.

Introduction

The [=verifiable credential=] ecosystem relies on the ability of [=verifiers=] to determine whether a particular [=issuer=] is recognized to perform a particular action, such as [=issuer|issuing=] a certain type of [=verifiable credential=]. Historically, this determination has often been made through out-of-band processes, bilateral agreements, or proprietary registries that are difficult to discover, query, or reason about in an automated way. As [=verifiable credentials=] are increasingly deployed across sectors such as education, healthcare, financial services, and government, the need for a standardized, interoperable mechanism to express and communicate recognition of entities that perform known actions has become critical. Without such a mechanism, ecosystem participants are forced to build bespoke "trust infrastructure", leading to fragmentation, increased integration costs, and barriers to cross-border and cross-sector interoperability.

This specification addresses the challenge of expressing recognized entities, such as people and organizations, and the actions they perform, such as issuing and verifying, in a decentralized manner by defining a data model that any entity can use to publish or share recognition information as a [=verifiable credential=]. Rather than mandating a single central registry, the model allows any entity — a government body, an industry consortium, a standards organization, or even a single individual — to issue a `RecognizedEntityCredential` asserting that they know of one or more entities that are recognized to perform specific actions. This design enables a web of overlapping, competing, and complementary "trust registries" to coexist, allowing ecosystem participants to choose the entities they trust and to reason across multiple registries. The specification also supports interoperability with existing "trust infrastructure", such as X.509 certificate authority lists and ETSI Trust Service Lists, allowing existing recognized entity information to be referenced and composed into new decentralized ecosystems.

This document builds on the [=verifiable credential=] data model to define a specific application rather than modifying the [[[VC-DATA-MODEL]]] itself. It uses the linked-data foundation of JSON-LD to add a small number of new types and properties alongside existing terms. A `RecognizedEntityCredential` is thus an ordinary [=verifiable credential=] — issued, presented, and verified using the same mechanisms as any other.

Readers who are interested in the variety of use cases supported by this specification are urged to read Section [[[#use-cases]]]. The remainder of this document is organized into the following sections:

  • Section [[[#use-cases]]] describes a set of use cases that motivate the design of this specification.
  • Section [[[#data-model]]] defines the core data model, including the `RecognizedEntity`, `RecognizedAction`, and `RecognizedEntityCredential` types along with their properties and worked examples.
  • Appendices [[[#security-considerations]]], [[[#privacy-considerations]]], and [[[#market-competition-considerations]]] discuss the security, privacy, and market competition considerations relevant to implementers of this specification.

A conforming document is any concrete expression of the data model that complies with the normative statements in this specification. Specifically, all relevant normative statements in Section of this document MUST be enforced.

A conforming processor is any algorithm realized as software and/or hardware that generates or consumes a conforming document. Conforming processors MUST produce errors when non-conforming documents are consumed.

This document also contains examples that contain JSON and JSON-LD content. Some of these examples contain characters that are invalid JSON, such as inline comments (`//`) and the use of ellipsis (`...`) to denote information that adds little value to the example. Implementers are cautioned to remove this content if they desire to use the information as valid JSON or JSON-LD.

Use Cases

This section describes a set of use cases that motivate the design of this specification.

Education

A national education authority wants to publish the set of universities and colleges that it recognizes as legitimate institutions within its jurisdiction. The authority issues a single [=recognized entity credential=] that lists each recognized institution along with descriptive information such as its common name, legal name, logo, and website. This enables software to display trusted information — such as the university's name, logo, and website — about a recognized institution to an interested party, having confirmed in an automated and cryptographically-verifiable way that the institution is recognized by the national authority, without contacting the authority directly.

Vital Records

A national association of vital records agencies wants to publish not just which entities it recognizes — such as a city, regional, or provincial agency — but which specific [=verifiable credentials=] each entity is recognized to issue. For example, a particular regional office is recognized to issue birth certificates, marriage certificates, and death certificates for its jurisdiction. The association issues a [=recognized entity credential=] in which each [=recognized entity=] is associated with a [=recognized action=] describing the type of vital record it might issue and the schema that record conforms to. A [=verifier=] receiving a birth certificate can then confirm both that its [=issuer=] is a recognized jurisdiction and that the jurisdiction was recognized to issue that particular type of vital record.

Cross-border Trade

Roughly five billion commercial invoices are exchanged annually among approximately 50 million trading entities worldwide. An exporter issues a commercial invoice as a [=verifiable credential=] and sends it to the importer. The importer, in turn, presents that invoice to the importing customs authority for border clearance and to a trade finance lender for documentary letter of credit settlement. Neither the customs authority nor the lender has a direct relationship with the exporter — they receive the invoice indirectly, through the importer, yet must determine whether the exporter is a legitimate registered entity.

Verification relies on two independent but linked credentials, issued at different times and with different lifecycles:

  • The commercial invoice [=verifiable credential=], issued by the exporter's DID — billions issued per year with a lifecycle of weeks to months.
  • A `RecognizedEntityCredential` issued by the national business register of the exporting economy to the exporter — millions valid at any time, with lifecycles spanning years to decades.

Upon receiving the invoice, the [=verifier=] resolves the exporter's DID, discovers the linked `RecognizedEntityCredential` issued by the national business register, and confirms that the DID in the invoice matches the subject of that credential. This gives the customs authority or the lender both document integrity — the invoice has not been tampered with — and identity integrity — the [=issuer=] is a registered entity in the exporting economy — enabling automated border clearance and automated trade finance due diligence without any prior knowledge of the exporter.

Product Conformity

A conformity assessment body (CAB) issues a certificate of conformity (CoC) as a [=verifiable credential=] attesting to the safety or quality performance of a specific product. The CoC accompanies the product through the supply chain to an importing market. However, the market surveillance authority in the importing country typically has no direct relationship with the CAB and cannot independently determine whether the certificate was issued by a genuinely accredited test laboratory.

When the national accreditation authority in the exporting country issues a `RecognizedEntityCredential` to the CAB — attesting to the CAB's accreditation scope — a linked verification chain becomes possible, analogous to the cross-border trade case above:

  • The certificate of conformity [=verifiable credential=], issued by the CAB's DID, asserting that a product meets specific safety or quality standards.
  • A `RecognizedEntityCredential` issued by the national accreditation authority to the CAB, attesting that the CAB is accredited to perform conformity assessments within a defined scope.

Upon receiving the CoC, the market surveillance authority resolves the CAB's DID, discovers the linked `RecognizedEntityCredential` issued by the national accreditation authority, and confirms that the DID in the certificate matches the subject of that credential. This gives the authority both document integrity — the CoC has not been tampered with — and identity integrity — the [=issuer=] is a genuinely accredited CAB — enabling pre-clearance of goods at the border without manual verification of the CAB's accreditation status.

Federated Identifiers

There are multiple examples of federated identifiers in common usage. International Standard Book Numbers (ISBNs), Legal Entity identifiers (LEIs), BIC numbers for shipping containers, and the Internet's domain name system. In each case, a central authority assigns a prefix to a registrar who then assigns a range of identifiers that extend that original prefix to a third party. The third party can then extend the character string further to create the complete identifier.

Presented with such an identifier in a credential, a relying party might want to verify that it has been constructed by a party known to the federated identifier scheme owner, in full conformance with its operating procedures.

An example of such a scheme is GS1, the organization behind many of the world's barcodes. A detailed description of its use case is presented in the Verifiable Credentials Use Cases document [[vc-use-cases]].

Data Model

The following sections outline the data model that is used by this specification for Verifiable Issuer and Verifier Lists.

The data model described in this section has been built using input from a variety of the prior art evaluated for this paper including input from the EBSI Trusted Issuer Registry, ETSI TS 119 612, eSSIF-Lab TRAIN, the Trust over IP Foundation Trust Registry Protocol, and Rebooting the Web of Trust input documents. The data model described in this section is capable of expressing many, but not all, of the concepts described in those other specifications.

The unified data model for this work can be represented as a list of service providers that represent entities or organizations that provide services such as credential issuance or validation. The data model also includes the details of the list operator description.

General Properties

The properties in this section can be added to objects found in a `RecognizedEntityCredential` as defined in Section [[[#recognizedentitycredential]]]. Each general property listed in this section is OPTIONAL; none of the values are required to be provided by an [=issuer=].

Property Description
id A URL that identifies the entity in a globally unambiguous way. The value for this property is defined in Section 4.4: Identifiers of the [[[VC-DATA-MODEL]]] specification.
type The type of the entity. The value for this property is defined in Section 4.5: Types of the [[[VC-DATA-MODEL]]] specification. The `type` property MUST be `RecognizedIssuer` if the entity is an [=issuer=] of [=verifiable credentials=].
name A human-readable name for the entity. The value for this property is defined in Section 4.6: Names and Descriptions of the [[[VC-DATA-MODEL]]] specification.
legalName The official legal name of an organization or entity, as registered with legal authorities, which can differ from the commonly used name. The value MUST be a one or more [=string=]s or [=language value object=]s, as described in Section 11.1: Language and Base Direction of the [[[VC-DATA-MODEL]]] specification. An entity that has an official legal name in more than one language can express each of them by providing an array of [=language value objects=].
image A link to a visual representation of the entity, such as a logo, photograph, or icon. The value MUST be a [=URL=], which includes values using the `data:` URL scheme as described in [[?RFC2397]].
url A URL pointing to the primary website or web resource associated with the entity. The value MUST be a [=URL=].
sameAs One or more URLs that refer to the same entity in other contexts or systems, enabling cross-reference and verification across different platforms. Each value MUST be a [=URL=].
description A human-readable description providing details about the entity. The value for this property is defined in Section 4.6: Names and Descriptions of the [[[VC-DATA-MODEL]]] specification.
digestMultibase One or more cryptographic digests used to verify the integrity of resources associated with the entity. The values for this property are defined in Section 5.3: Integrity of Related Resources of the [[[VC-DATA-MODEL]]] specification.

RecognizedEntity

A recognized entity is any entity that is recognized by an [=issuer=] of a `RecognizedEntityCredential` to perform a specific action.

Property Description
id A URL that identifies the entity in a globally unambiguous way. The value for this property is defined in Section 4.4: Identifiers of the [[[VC-DATA-MODEL]]] specification.
type The `type` property MUST be `RecognizedEntity`. The value for this property is defined in Section 4.5: Types of the [[[VC-DATA-MODEL]]] specification.
recognizedTo A specific action that the [=recognized entity=] is expected to perform as defined in Section [[[#recognizedaction]]]. This property may occur more than once if the [=recognized entity=] is expected to perform more than one action.
recognizedIn An object that contains a reference to a document of recognized entities that contains this particular [=recognized entity=] as well as the actions it is known to perform. The `id` value of the object MUST be a [=URL=]. The `type` value of the object MUST conform to the type value space defined in the [[[VC-DATA-MODEL]]] specification and SHOULD be `ExternalEntityList` or `RecognizedEntityCredential`.
registration One or more objects, each of which describes how the [=recognized entity=] is recorded in a register maintained by the [=issuer=], such as a national business register, an accreditation register, or the allocation records of a federated identifier scheme. Each object is expected to carry properties, such as the identifier the register assigned to the [=recognized entity=] and the scope of the `registration`, drawn from vocabularies defined by the ecosystem in which the register operates. Section [[[#un-grid-cross-border-trade-example]]] demonstrates a `registration` expressed using properties defined by the UNTP Digital Identity Anchor [[?UNTP-DIA]]. This property might occur more than once if the [=recognized entity=] holds more than one `registration` with the [=issuer=].

Properties from Section [[[#general-properties]]] can be included in addition to the properties above.

A `recognizedIn` entry with a `type` property value of `ExternalEntityList` SHOULD conform to a valid entity list specification. The origin of the list URL referenced by the `id` property in this case establishes the root of trust for the list. Examples of this are the X.509 Certificate Authority List, as specified in [[[RFC5280]]] and the European Union ETSI Trust Service List, as specified in [[[ETSI-TRUST-LISTS]]]. For the `ExternalEntityList` type, [=verifiers=] and [=credential=] consumers need to fetch the entity list resource referenced by the `id` property in order to determine the entity list specification that the resource complies with, determine if it supports that specification, and process/terminate accordingly. A list with a `type` property value of `RecognizedEntityCredential` MUST conform to [[[VC-RECOGNIZED-ENTITIES]]].

The `registration` and `recognizedTo` properties are independent of one another; either, both, or neither can be expressed for a given [=recognized entity=]. A `registration` describes the standing of the [=recognized entity=] in a register that the [=issuer=] maintains, such as its business registration number and legal form, the scope of its accreditation, or the identifier range it has been allocated. The `recognizedTo` property describes an action whose outputs the [=issuer=] recognizes, such as [=verifiable credentials=] that conform to a particular schema.

RecognizedAction

A recognized action is an action that a [=recognized entity=] is expected to perform.

Property Description
type The `type` property MUST be `RecognizedAction`.
action A [=string=] that specifies the name of the action to be performed such as `issue` or `verify`.
recognizedBy A [=URL=], or object containing properties from Section [[[#general-properties]]], of the entity that performed the task of recognizing.
credentialValidator The value of the `credentialValidator` property MUST be one or more [=credential validators=], as defined in Section [[[#credential-validators]]], that provide [=verifiers=] with enough information to determine whether a [=verifiable credential=] produced by the [=recognized entity=] meets the constraints of the recognizing authority. If more than one [=credential validator=] is present, the [=verifiable credential=] MUST satisfy every one of them.
validFrom The earliest point in time at which the [=recognized action=] is valid, that is, the date and time from which the [=recognized entity=] is recognized to perform the action. The value MUST be an XML Schema dateTime value expressed in UTC with no sub-second precision, for example `2025-01-01T00:00:00Z`. This property is OPTIONAL; if absent, the validity period of the enclosing [=recognized entity credential=] applies. This point in time is inclusive. A [=verifier=] checks it against the evaluation time, as described in Section [[[#recognition-validation]]].
validUntil The latest point in time at which the [=recognized action=] is valid, that is, the date and time after which the [=recognized entity=] is no longer recognized to perform the action. The value MUST be an XML Schema dateTime value expressed in UTC with no sub-second precision, for example `2030-01-01T00:00:00Z`. This property is OPTIONAL; if absent, the validity period of the enclosing [=recognized entity credential=] applies. This point in time is inclusive. A [=verifier=] checks it against the evaluation time, as described in Section [[[#recognition-validation]]].

RecognizedEntityCredential

When a recognized entity credential is published, it MUST be a conforming [=verifiable credential=], as defined in [[[VC-DATA-MODEL-2.0]]], that expresses the data model specified in the section that follows. It describes the format of a [=verifiable credential=] that encapsulates the recognized entities.

A recognized entity is expressed inside a [=verifiable credential=], enabling a [=holder=] to provide it directly to a [=verifier=]. This mechanism, sometimes called "certificate stapling", increases privacy for the [=holder=] by ensuring that the [=verifier=] does not need to contact the [=issuer=] to retrieve the recognized entity credential. Still, a [=verifier=] might choose to ignore the [=holder=]-provided [=recognized entity credential=], even when its authenticity is verifiable, if, for instance, it desires a more recent version of the [=recognized entity credential=].

Property Description
id A [=verifiable credential=] that contains a set of recognized entities MAY express an `id` property to make its retrieval easier for other systems.
type A [=verifiable credential=] that contains a set of recognized entities MUST express a `type` property that includes the `RecognizedEntityCredential` value.
issuer The [=issuer=] of the [=verifiable credential=] as defined in the Verifiable Credentials Data Model specification in Section 4.76: Issuer. This object, whose type is `RecognizedIssuer`, MAY also include other properties listed in Section [[[#general-properties]]]. This object MAY include a `recognizedIn` property, as defined in Section [[[#recognizedentity]]], that references a document of recognized entities in which the [=issuer=] itself appears; this enables the [=credential-based discovery=] process described in Section [[[#credential-based-discovery]]], allowing a [=verifier=] to traverse the recognition hierarchy until it reaches an [=issuer=] it recognizes.
validFrom The earliest point in time at which the credential is valid. This property is defined in the Verifiable Credentials Data Model specification in Section 4.6: Validity Period.
validUntil The latest point in time at which the credential is valid. This property is defined in the Verifiable Credentials Data Model specification in Section 4.6: Validity Period.
credentialSubject A set of one or more `RecognizedEntity` objects as defined in Section [[[#recognizedentity]]].

Credential Validators

A credential validator is a value of the `credentialValidator` property of a [=recognized action=]. It links to a file that a [=verifier=] uses to determine whether a [=verifiable credential=] produced by a [=recognized entity=] meets the constraints of the recognizing authority. The media type of that file determines what kind of [=credential validator=] it is, and therefore how a [=verifier=] evaluates it, as described in Section [[[#credential-validation]]].

Property Description
id The `id` property is REQUIRED. Its value MUST be a [=URL=] that links to the file used to validate the [=verifiable credential=]. The value MAY be a `data:` URL, as described in [[?RFC2397]], that embeds the file.
type The `type` property MUST be `CredentialValidator`.
mediaType The `mediaType` property is REQUIRED. Its value is the media type of the file identified by `id`, as defined in Section 5.3: Integrity of Related Resources of the [[[VC-DATA-MODEL]]] specification, and identifies the kind of [=credential validator=]. This specification defines the `application/schema+json` and `text/javascript` media types for [=credential validators=], in the sections below.

A [=credential validator=] SHOULD provide a cryptographic hash of the file identified by `id`, using one of the mechanisms described in Section 5.3: Integrity of Related Resources of the [[[VC-DATA-MODEL]]] specification. A hash binds the [=credential validator=] to one exact version of the file. Any change to the file, including a change to whitespace or comments, produces a different hash.

JSON Schema

A JSON Schema credential validator is a [=credential validator=] whose `mediaType` is `application/schema+json`. Its `id` links to a JSON Schema [[JSON-SCHEMA]]. A [=verifier=] evaluates it by validating the [=verifiable credential=] against that schema. A [=JSON Schema credential validator=] examines only the [=verifiable credential=] being validated.

    "type": "CredentialValidator",

JavaScript

[=JavaScript credential validators=] are at risk and might be removed from this specification before it becomes a W3C Recommendation if there are not at least two independent implementations of them.

A JavaScript credential validator is a [=credential validator=] whose `mediaType` is `text/javascript`. Its `id` links to JavaScript code that a [=verifier=] runs to determine whether a [=verifiable credential=] meets the constraints of the recognizing authority. The code receives the [=verifiable credential=] being validated and, in its options, the recognition chain, so it can compare values across [=verifiable credentials=] in that chain. For example, it can confirm that an identifier in one [=verifiable credential=] begins with a prefix that a [=recognized entity credential=] higher in the chain licensed to its [=issuer=]. A [=verifier=] runs that code as described in Section [[[#javascript-validator-processing]]].

Algorithms

This section describes algorithms that a [=verifier=] can use when processing [=verifiable credentials=] and [=recognized entity credentials=] defined by this specification. These algorithms separate discovery from validation.

Discovery locates the [=recognized entity credentials=] that might establish the recognition of an [=issuer=]. Section [[[#credential-based-discovery]]] and Section [[[#identifier-based-discovery]]] describe two mechanisms for doing so. A [=verifier=] MAY use any other mechanism, including receiving the [=recognized entity credentials=] directly from a [=holder=] as described in Section [[[#holder-based-delivery]]].

Locating a [=recognized entity credential=] does not establish that an [=issuer=] is recognized. A [=verifier=] that skips the checks in Section [[[#recognition-validation]]] might accept a [=verifiable credential=] that is unrelated to the [=issuer=] in question, or one whose recognition chain never reaches an anchor that the [=verifier=] accepts.

Recognition validation, described in Section [[[#recognition-validation]]], determines whether the discovered [=verifiable credentials=] establish recognition. It applies the same way regardless of how they were discovered.

When identifiers for [=subjects=], such as [=issuers=], are compared for set membership or equivalence, simple string comparison is used. While it is possible to calculate the canonical form of an identifier in a scheme before performing a comparison, the canoncalization rules can vary between identifier schemes, leading to high levels of implementation complexity. To reduce the implementation burden, a choice was made to use simple string comparison instead. Simple string comparison can sometimes lead to non-equivalence between identifiers that are canonically the same, such as two HTTPS URLs that differ in a query parameter that results in the same document. This downside is the trade-off made to keep implementation burden low for identifier comparison.

Both processes assume that each [=verifiable credential=] has already been validated individually. That is: its proof verifies; it is within its validity period at the evaluation time described in Section [[[#recognition-validation]]]; and its status, if any, has been checked, according to Section 7.1: Verification and Appendix A: Validation of the [[[VC-DATA-MODEL]]] specification. Those processes apply to any [=verifiable credential=] and are not restated here.

These algorithms fetch things from the web at several points. The [=URLs=] they fetch come from [=verifiable credentials=] and identifier documents that have not been checked yet. A retrieval policy sets limits on what a [=verifier=] will fetch. The [=verifier=]'s HTTP client and identifier resolver apply those limits.

A [=retrieval policy=] says which [=URL=] schemes are allowed. It says which addresses are allowed. It blocks private addresses, such as loopback, link-local, and addresses inside a local network. A name is looked up before a request is sent, so the policy checks the address that the name points to. It checks each redirect the same way. It also limits how many redirects are followed.

These algorithms check a resource after fetching it. By then the request has already arrived, and whatever it did there is done. A proof check tells the [=verifier=] whether to trust the answer. It cannot take the request back. Only a [=retrieval policy=] acts before the request goes out.

Credential-based Discovery

Credential-based discovery is the process by which a [=verifier=] that receives a [=verifiable credential=] from an unknown [=issuer=] locates a [=recognized entity credential=] that recognizes that [=issuer=], and, if necessary, traverses a hierarchy of [=recognized entity credentials=] until it reaches one whose own [=issuer=] the [=verifier=] recognizes.

The mechanism relies on the `recognizedIn` property. When present on the `issuer` object of a [=verifiable credential=], including on the `issuer` object of a [=recognized entity credential=], the `recognizedIn` property references a [=verifiable credential=] of recognized entities that lists that [=issuer=] as a [=recognized entity=]. A [=verifier=] can fetch the referenced [=verifiable credential=] and inspect the [=issuer=] of that [=verifiable credential=]. If the [=verifier=] recognizes that [=issuer=], discovery succeeds. If it does not, the [=verifier=] can follow the `recognizedIn` property on the fetched [=verifiable credential=]'s `issuer` object to move one level up the hierarchy, repeating the process until it reaches an [=issuer=] it recognizes or a configured limit is reached.

A [=holder=] MAY pre-fetch the [=recognized entity credentials=] along this hierarchy and provide them to the [=verifier=] alongside the [=verifiable credential=], using the "certificate stapling" mechanism described in Section [[[#recognizedentitycredential]]]. When the [=holder=] provides these [=verifiable credentials=], the [=verifier=] does not need to fetch them itself, provided that the [=verifier=] is satisfied with the freshness of the provided [=verifiable credentials=]. A [=verifier=] that requires more recent information MAY reject the [=holder=]-provided [=verifiable credentials=] or ignore them and fetch the [=recognized entity credentials=] directly.

To perform [=credential-based discovery=] on a [=verifiable credential=] (credential) against a configured set of recognized [=issuers=] (recognizedIssuers) and an optional set of [=holder=]-provided [=verifiable credentials=] (providedDocuments), a [=verifier=] runs the following algorithm. The algorithm returns a chain, which is a list of [=verifiable credentials=] beginning with credential, followed by each [=recognized entity credential=] found by following `recognizedIn` references. The [=verifier=] then runs the algorithm in Section [[[#recognition-validation]]] on the returned chain.

This algorithm stops once it reaches an [=issuer=] in recognizedIssuers, because there is no reason to retrieve more [=verifiable credentials=] after finding a candidate anchor. That test bounds retrieval, but it does not establish recognition. A chain that never reaches a member of recognizedIssuers is still returned, and Section [[[#recognition-validation]]] rejects it.

maxDepth bounds this algorithm on its own. When [=identifier-based discovery=] invokes it, its retrievals also count against that caller's maxDuration, and exhausting the budget raises an error from the caller's operation.

  1. Let current be credential and let chain be a list containing credential.
  2. Let maxDepth be the maximum recognition chain depth the [=verifier=] is configured to traverse. If the length of chain exceeds maxDepth, raise a `LIMIT_EXCEEDED_ERROR`.
  3. Let issuer be the value of the `issuer` property of current, and let issuerId be its identifier.
  4. If issuerId is a member of recognizedIssuers, a candidate anchor has been reached, so return chain.
  5. If issuer does not contain a `recognizedIn` property, the recognition hierarchy cannot be traversed any further, so return chain.
  6. Let listReference be the value of the `recognizedIn` property and let listId be its `id`.
  7. Let list be the [=recognized entity credential=] identified by listId, obtained from providedDocuments if present and sufficiently fresh, or otherwise retrieved from listId. Apply the [=retrieval policy=] to that retrieval. If list cannot be obtained, or the [=retrieval policy=] blocks the retrieval, raise a `RETRIEVAL_ERROR` identifying listId.
  8. Append list to chain, set current to list, and return to step 2 to evaluate the [=issuer=] of the list.

A [=verifier=] that does not directly recognize the state university can follow that `recognizedIn` reference to the state's list of recognized universities, issued by the state department of education, to determine whether the state university is itself a [=recognized entity=].

If the [=verifier=] recognizes the state department of education that issued that list, discovery succeeds; otherwise, the [=verifier=] continues up the recognition hierarchy via the list's own `issuer` object until it reaches an [=issuer=] it recognizes, thereby completing the recognition chain.

Identifier-based Discovery

Identifier-based discovery is the process by which a [=verifier=] that receives a [=verifiable credential=] from an unknown [=issuer=] starts from the [=issuer=]'s identifier, resolves that identifier to a [=controlled identifier document=], and uses a service declared in that document to retrieve a [=verifiable presentation=] that might contain a [=recognized entity credential=] recognizing the [=issuer=]. Whereas [=credential-based discovery=] relies on a `recognizedIn` reference being present on the `issuer` object, identifier-based discovery makes no such assumption; it instead obtains recognition information by dereferencing the [=issuer=]'s identifier itself.

The mechanism relies on the [=issuer=] publishing recognition information at a service endpoint that is discoverable from its identifier. The [=issuer=]'s identifier, typically expressed as the `id` of the `issuer` object (for example, `issuer.id`), is a [=URL=] that can be resolved or dereferenced to a [=controlled identifier document=], such as a [=DID document=]. That document MAY declare a service whose `type` array includes both `WhoisService` and `PathService`; the service's `serviceEndpoint` identifies the location at which the [=issuer=] publishes a [=verifiable presentation=] describing itself. The published [=verifiable presentation=] is signed by the [=issuer=] and contains [=verifiable credentials=] for which the [=issuer=]'s identifier is the `credentialSubject`. Among these MAY be a [=recognized entity credential=] in which the [=issuer=] appears as a [=recognized entity=].

A [=verifier=] that obtains a [=recognized entity credential=] this way processes it exactly as in [=credential-based discovery=]. If the [=verifier=] recognizes the [=issuer=] of that [=recognized entity credential=], discovery succeeds. If it does not, and the [=recognized entity credential=] carries a `recognizedIn` property on its own `issuer` object, the [=verifier=] MAY continue with [=credential-based discovery=] to traverse the recognition hierarchy until it reaches an [=issuer=] it recognizes.

The diagram below shows the [=identifier-based discovery=] process from the [=verifier=]'s perspective. The same steps repeat at each level of the hierarchy until a recognized trust anchor is reached.

To perform [=identifier-based discovery=] on a [=verifiable credential=] (credential) against a configured set of recognized [=issuers=] (recognizedIssuers), within a time budget (maxDuration), a [=verifier=] runs the following algorithm. The algorithm returns a list of candidate chains, where each chain is a list of [=verifiable credentials=] beginning with credential. The [=verifier=] then runs the algorithm in Section [[[#recognition-validation]]] on the returned chains, which describes how to select among their results.

maxDuration bounds the total elapsed time of one discovery operation, measured from when the algorithm starts. It covers all work, including that of a nested [=credential-based discovery=]. maxDepth bounds one chain; maxDuration bounds the operation. Exceeding it raises a `LIMIT_EXCEEDED_ERROR`.

Whether the budget is exhausted depends on network conditions and on traversal order, so the same [=verifiable presentation=] can succeed for one [=verifier=] and fail for another. A [=verifier=] whose budget runs out has established nothing about the [=issuer=]. The error says the budget was exceeded, not that recognition failed, so a [=verifier=] can retry with a larger maxDuration.

  1. Let chains be an empty list.
  2. Let issuer be the value of the `issuer` property of credential, and let issuerId be its identifier.
  3. Resolve or dereference issuerId to obtain a controlled identifier document, idDocument. Apply the [=retrieval policy=] to any retrieval that resolution performs. If issuerId cannot be resolved, or the [=retrieval policy=] blocks the retrieval, raise a `RETRIEVAL_ERROR` identifying issuerId.
  4. Let service be a service in the `service` array of idDocument whose `type` includes both `WhoisService` and `PathService`. If no such service is present, return chains; identifier-based discovery is not supported for this [=issuer=].
  5. Let endpoint be the `serviceEndpoint` of service and retrieve the [=verifiable presentation=], presentation, published at endpoint. The [=issuer=] controls endpoint, and nothing has been verified at this point, so apply the [=retrieval policy=] to this retrieval. If retrieval fails, or the [=retrieval policy=] blocks it, return chains.
  6. Verify the proof on presentation and confirm that it is bound to issuerId. If verification fails, raise a `CRYPTOGRAPHIC_SECURITY_ERROR`.
  7. Let candidates be the [=recognized entity credentials=] contained in presentation in which issuerId appears as the `id` of a [=recognized entity=] in the `credentialSubject`. If candidates is empty, return chains.
  8. For each list in candidates, do the following. If the [=issuer=] of list is a member of recognizedIssuers, append the chain consisting of credential followed by list to chains. Otherwise, if the `issuer` object of list contains a `recognizedIn` property, perform [=credential-based discovery=] on list against recognizedIssuers and append the chain consisting of credential followed by the elements of the returned chain to chains. If the elapsed time since this algorithm started exceeds maxDuration, raise a `LIMIT_EXCEEDED_ERROR` identifying maxDuration and the [=URL=] being retrieved, if any.
  9. Return chains.

Recognition Validation

Recognition validation is the process by which a [=verifier=] determines whether a chain of [=verifiable credentials=] establishes that the [=issuer=] of a [=verifiable credential=] is recognized, and whether it is recognized to perform a particular action. The process does not depend on how the chain was obtained. A chain returned by [=credential-based discovery=], a chain returned by [=identifier-based discovery=], and a chain delivered by a [=holder=] as described in Section [[[#holder-based-delivery]]] are all validated the same way.

This algorithm checks the relationships between the [=verifiable credentials=] in the chain. It does not check the internal validity of any individual [=verifiable credential=], which Section [[[#algorithms]]] assumes has already been established.

To perform [=recognition validation=] on a chain (chain) produced by any discovery mechanism, for a specific action (action, for example `issue`), against a configured set of recognized [=issuers=] (recognizedIssuers), at an evaluation time (evaluationDateTime), a [=verifier=] runs the following algorithm. The algorithm returns a result with two boolean outcomes: recognized, indicating whether the [=issuer=] of the first [=verifiable credential=] in chain is ultimately recognized by a member of recognizedIssuers, and actionRecognized, indicating whether that [=issuer=] is recognized to perform action and, where a [=recognized action=] declares a `credentialValidator`, whether the [=verifiable credential=] conforms to it.

evaluationDateTime is the point in time at which recognition is evaluated. A [=verifier=] normally sets it to the current date and time. A [=verifier=] that asks whether recognition held at some past date sets it to that date instead. Every date comparison in this algorithm uses evaluationDateTime. Validity period endpoints are inclusive. An instant equal to a `validFrom` or a `validUntil` value is within the period that value bounds.

  1. Let result be a structure with recognized set to `false` and actionRecognized set to `false`.
  2. If chain contains fewer than two [=verifiable credentials=], return result. A recognition chain consists of at least a [=verifiable credential=] and a [=recognized entity credential=] that recognizes its [=issuer=].
  3. Let maxDepth be the maximum recognition chain depth the [=verifier=] is configured to accept. If the length of chain exceeds maxDepth, return result.
  4. Let seen be an empty set. For each [=verifiable credential=] in chain, let id be the identifier of its [=issuer=]. If id is already a member of seen, the chain contains a cycle, so return result. Otherwise, add id to seen.
  5. Let window be the period during which every [=verifiable credential=] in chain was valid at the same time. To determine it, let window start at the latest `validFrom` property in chain and end at the earliest `validUntil` property in chain. A [=verifiable credential=] that expresses no `validFrom` property does not constrain the start, and one that expresses no `validUntil` property does not constrain the end. If window ends before it starts, no such period exists, so return result. If evaluationDateTime falls outside window, at least one [=verifiable credential=] in chain is not valid at the evaluation time, so return result.
  6. For each adjacent pair of [=verifiable credentials=] in chain, where subject is the earlier [=verifiable credential=] and list is the later one, confirm that list is a [=recognized entity credential=] and that the `credentialSubject` of list contains a [=recognized entity=] whose `id` is equal, using simple string comparison, to the identifier of the [=issuer=] of subject. If it does not, the two [=verifiable credentials=] are unrelated, so return result.
  7. Confirm that the [=issuer=] of the final [=verifiable credential=] in chain is a member of recognizedIssuers. If it is not, the chain does not terminate at an anchor that the [=verifier=] recognizes, so return result. Otherwise, set result.recognized to `true`.
  8. Let credential be the first [=verifiable credential=] in chain, let list be the second, and let entity be the [=recognized entity=] in the `credentialSubject` of list whose `id` is the identifier of the [=issuer=] of credential. Set result.actionRecognized to `true` if any [=recognized action=] declared by the `recognizedTo` property of entity meets all of the following conditions.
    1. Its `action` value is equal to action.
    2. Its validity period includes evaluationDateTime. If it expresses neither a `validFrom` nor a `validUntil` property, the validity period of the enclosing [=recognized entity credential=] applies. If it expresses one but not the other, the enclosing credential's corresponding property applies to the unexpressed value.
    3. It declares no `credentialValidator`, or performing [=credential validation=], as described in Section [[[#credential-validation]]], on credential with the [=credential validators=] declared by its `credentialValidator` property, and with options whose `chain` value is every item of chain after the first, whose `action` value is action, and whose `evaluationDateTime` value is evaluationDateTime, returns a result whose valid value is `true`.
  9. Return result.

A result in which recognized is `true` and actionRecognized is `false` means that the [=issuer=] is recognized by an anchor that the [=verifier=] recognizes, but was not recognized to perform the action in question, or produced a [=verifiable credential=] that does not conform to the schema that the [=recognized action=] requires. A [=verifier=] is expected to treat that outcome as a failure whenever the action matters to the transaction.

A [=recognized entity credential=] that lacks a `recognizedTo` property for a given [=recognized entity=] asserts that the entity is recognized, without constraining the actions it performs. A [=verifier=] that requires an explicit action to be recognized treats such a [=recognized entity credential=] as producing an actionRecognized value of `false`.

A discovery mechanism can produce more than one candidate chain, as [=identifier-based discovery=] does when an [=issuer=] publishes [=recognized entity credentials=] from more than one recognizing authority. A [=verifier=] runs this algorithm on each candidate chain and uses the first result in which both recognized and actionRecognized are `true`, falling back to a result in which only recognized is `true`.

Credential Validation

Credential validation is the process by which a [=verifier=] determines whether a [=verifiable credential=] meets the constraints declared by the `credentialValidator` property of a [=recognized action=]. Section [[[#recognition-validation]]] runs it as its final check, after it has walked the chain, confirmed that each adjacent pair of [=verifiable credentials=] in the chain is related, and confirmed that the chain ends at an anchor that the [=verifier=] recognizes. The proof on every [=verifiable credential=] in the chain has already been verified, as described in Section [[[#algorithms]]].

Credential validation does not change how a chain is discovered, walked, or verified, and the kind of [=credential validator=] in use does not change it either. Each [=credential validator=] is evaluated against the already verified chain using the processing defined for its `mediaType`. Credential validation always fails when a [=verifier=] does not support the `mediaType` of a [=credential validator=].

The inputs to every [=credential validator=] are the [=verifiable credential=] being validated and a [=map=] of options. The options carry all of the other information used in the evaluation. They contain the following properties.

Property Description
chain Present if the [=verifiable credential=] is validated as part of a recognition chain. An array containing the rest of that chain, which is every [=verifiable credential=] in it except the one being validated, each expressed as the JSON object the [=verifier=] received, in the order defined in Section [[[#recognition-validation]]]. The [=verifiable credential=] being validated is passed separately, so the array starts at the second item of the chain. Its first item, `chain[0]`, is the [=recognized entity credential=] that declares the [=credential validator=], and its last item is the [=recognized entity credential=] issued by the anchor that the [=verifier=] recognizes. The [=verifier=] has verified the proof on every item.
action The [=string=] value of the `action` property of the [=recognized action=] being evaluated, such as `issue`.
evaluationDateTime The point in time at which recognition is evaluated, as described in Section [[[#recognition-validation]]], expressed as an XML Schema dateTime value in UTC, for example `2026-11-04T00:00:00Z`.

Other specifications MAY define additional options. A [=credential validator=] MUST throw an `UNRECOGNIZED_OPTION_ERROR` if it does not recognize an option.

To perform [=credential validation=] on a [=verifiable credential=] (credential) with a list of [=credential validators=] (validators) and a [=map=] of options (options), a [=verifier=] runs the following algorithm. The algorithm returns a result containing a boolean valid, a list of errors, and a list of warnings.

  1. Let result be a structure with valid set to `true`, and errors and warnings each set to an empty list.
  2. If validators is a single object, treat it as a list containing that object.
  3. For each validator in validators, evaluate it using the processing defined for the value of its `mediaType` property:
    1. If the `type` of validator is not `CredentialValidator`, append an `CREDENTIAL_VALIDATION_ERROR` identifying the `type` to result.errors, set result.valid to `false`, and continue with the next validator.
    2. If the `mediaType` is `application/schema+json`, set result.valid to `false` if credential does not conform to the JSON Schema [[JSON-SCHEMA]] identified by the `id` property of validator.
    3. If the `mediaType` is `text/javascript`, let validatorResult be the result of running the algorithm in Section [[[#javascript-validator-processing]]] on validator, credential, and options. Append the items of validatorResult.errors to result.errors and the items of validatorResult.warnings to result.warnings. If validatorResult.valid is not `true`, set result.valid to `false`.
    4. Otherwise, the [=verifier=] does not support the `mediaType`. Append an `CREDENTIAL_VALIDATION_ERROR` identifying the `mediaType` to result.errors, and set result.valid to `false`.
  4. Return result.

Section [[[#recognition-validation]]] performs [=credential validation=] only after it has confirmed that the chain ends at an anchor that the [=verifier=] recognizes and that every adjacent pair in the chain is related. A [=verifier=] therefore retrieves and evaluates a [=credential validator=], including running any code it identifies, only when it was declared by an [=issuer=] whose recognition traces back to a recognition root known to the [=verifier=].

JavaScript Validator Processing

This section defines how [=credential validation=] evaluates a [=JavaScript credential validator=] against an already verified chain. The code is written by a third party and is downloaded via a [=recognized entity credential=]. Since the [=verifier=] does not always know the third party, it treats the downloaded code as untrusted and runs it in a sandbox that cannot reach the network, read the [=verifier=]'s own state, or learn anything else outside the sandbox. A [=verifier=] MAY use an algorithm other than the one below if its security and privacy outcomes, and its result, are the same.

The following terminology is used to describe the sandbox:

validation host
The [=verifier=] software that retrieves the [=validator code=], creates the sandbox, sends the inputs into the sandbox, and receives the result.
validator code
The JavaScript code identified by the `id` property of a [=JavaScript credential validator=].
wrapper code
Code that the [=validation host=] places in the sandbox alongside the [=validator code=]. It loads the [=validator code=], receives the inputs from the [=validation host=], invokes the [=validator code=], and returns its result to the [=validation host=].

The [=validator code=] MUST be an ECMAScript module, as defined in [[ECMASCRIPT]], whose default export is a function.

A sandbox that runs [=validator code=] SHOULD meet the following security requirements.

  • It prevents the [=validator code=] from making network requests of any kind, including importing other modules from a [=URL=] that is not a `data:` URL.
  • It prevents the [=validator code=] from reading or modifying any state of the [=validation host=], including its memory, storage, cookies, documents, and key material. The [=validator code=] receives copies of its inputs, not references to the [=validation host=]'s objects.
  • It provides no storage that persists after the sandbox is destroyed, and it is never reused. Each invocation of [=validator code=] starts in a new sandbox, so that one evaluation cannot influence another, and so that [=validator code=] cannot track which recognition chains a [=verifier=] evaluates.
  • It lets the [=validation host=] terminate the [=validator code=] at any time, without the cooperation of the [=validator code=], so that the [=validation host=] can enforce limits on running time and memory.

A JavaScript environment that shares a heap or an event loop with the [=validation host=], such as the `vm` module of Node.js, does not meet the requirements above. Environments that can meet them include a separate operating system process, an isolate provided by a library such as `isolated-vm`, and a JavaScript engine compiled to WebAssembly.

To run a [=JavaScript credential validator=] (validator) on a [=verifiable credential=] (credential) with a [=map=] of options (options), as described in Section [[[#credential-validation]]], a [=verifier=] runs the following algorithm. The algorithm returns a result containing a boolean valid, a list of errors, and a list of warnings. Each error and warning is a ProblemDetails object.

  1. Let result be a structure with valid set to `false`, and errors and warnings each set to an empty list.
  2. If the [=verifier=] cannot provide a sandbox that meets the requirements in this section, append an `CREDENTIAL_VALIDATION_ERROR` to result.errors and return result.
  3. Let codeUrl be the value of the `id` property of validator. If codeUrl is a `data:` URL, let code be the bytes it encodes. Otherwise, let code be a copy of the [=validator code=] that the [=verifier=] has cached, or, if it has none, the bytes retrieved from codeUrl. Apply the [=retrieval policy=] to that retrieval. If code cannot be obtained, or the [=retrieval policy=] blocks the retrieval, append a `RETRIEVAL_ERROR` identifying codeUrl to result.errors and return result.
  4. If validator provides a cryptographic hash of the [=validator code=], ensure that the cryptographic hash of code matches the one provided by the [=issuer=], as described in Section 5.3: Integrity of Related Resources of the [[[VC-DATA-MODEL]]] specification. If they do not match, append a `CRYPTOGRAPHIC_SECURITY_ERROR` identifying codeUrl to result.errors and return result. No part of code is parsed or run before this check succeeds.
  5. Let maxDuration and maxMemory be the limits on running time and memory that the [=verifier=] is configured to allow [=validator code=]. Create a new sandbox, place the [=wrapper code=] and code in it, and start measuring elapsed time and memory use. If either limit is exceeded at any point before the sandbox is destroyed, destroy the sandbox, append a `LIMIT_EXCEEDED_ERROR` identifying the limit that was exceeded to result.errors, and return result.
  6. Serialize credential and options to JSON [[RFC8259]] and send them into the sandbox. The [=wrapper code=] parses them, loads code as an ECMAScript module, and calls the module's default export, the [=validation function=], with the parsed credential as its first argument and the parsed options as its second. If the function returns a promise, the [=wrapper code=] waits for it to settle.
  7. If code fails to parse as an ECMAScript module, or its default export is not a function, destroy the sandbox, append an `CREDENTIAL_VALIDATION_ERROR` to result.errors, and return result.
  8. If the function throws an exception or returns a promise that rejects, destroy the sandbox. If the exception has a `type` property whose value is a [=URL=], such as an `UNRECOGNIZED_OPTION_ERROR`, append a ProblemDetails object with that `type` and with the exception's message as its `detail` to result.errors. Otherwise, append an `CREDENTIAL_VALIDATION_ERROR` to result.errors. Return result.
  9. The [=wrapper code=] serializes the value the function returned to JSON and sends it to the [=validation host=]. Destroy the sandbox. Let output be the result of parsing that JSON.
  10. If output does not have the form of a [=validation result=], as described in Section [[[#javascript-credential-validation-function]]], append an `CREDENTIAL_VALIDATION_ERROR` to result.errors and return result.
  11. Set result.valid to the `valid` value of output. Append the items of the `errors` value of output, if any, to result.errors, and the items of its `warnings` value, if any, to result.warnings.
  12. If result.errors is not empty, set result.valid to `false`.
  13. Return result.
JavaScript Credential Validation Function

The validation function is the default export of the [=validator code=]. The [=wrapper code=] calls it as follows: 

validate(credential, options) → result

The function MAY be asynchronous, in which case it returns a promise that resolves to result. Its arguments and its return value are described below.

credential
The [=verifiable credential=] being validated, expressed as the JSON object the [=verifier=] received. The [=verifier=] has verified its proof before the function is called, so the function does not verify proofs.
options
The [=map=] of options defined in Section [[[#credential-validation]]], such as `chain`, `action`, and `evaluationDateTime`, expressed as JSON.
result
A validation result, which is a [=map=] containing the following properties.
Property Description
valid REQUIRED. A [=boolean=] that is `true` if credential meets the constraints that the [=validator code=] checks, and `false` otherwise.
errors OPTIONAL. An array of ProblemDetails objects, each describing a constraint that credential does not meet. If `valid` is `true`, this array MUST be empty or absent.
warnings OPTIONAL. An array of ProblemDetails objects, each describing a condition that a [=verifier=] might want to know about but that does not cause validation to fail.

Each item in `errors` and `warnings` MUST conform to Section 7.1.4: Problem Details of the [[[VC-DATA-MODEL]]] specification. An item that does not conform causes the algorithm in Section [[[#javascript-validator-processing]]] to fail.

The [=validation function=] MUST NOT depend on any input other than credential and options. In particular, it uses `options.evaluationDateTime` rather than the current time, and does not use random numbers, so that every [=verifier=] that evaluates the same credential and options obtains the same result.

The ProblemDetails objects in a [=validation result=] are written by the author of the [=validator code=]. A [=verifier=] that shows their contents to a person displays them as plain text, and does not interpret them as markup or as instructions.

The example below shows the [=validator code=] referenced by the [=JavaScript credential validator=] in Section [[[#javascript-credential-validators]]]. For a chain consisting of a key credential issued by Healthy Tots, the [=recognized entity credential=] issued by GS1 Utopia, and the [=recognized entity credential=] issued by the GS1 Global Office, it reads the GS1 Company Prefix `9506000` licensed to Healthy Tots and the GS1 Prefix `950` licensed to GS1 Utopia. It then confirms that the GTIN `09506000134352` begins with `9506000` after its leading indicator digit, and that `9506000` begins with `950`. If the chain ends at an anchor other than the GS1 Global Office, the function reports a warning, because the GS1 Prefix in the chain was then not checked against the GS1 Global Office's own allocation. 

/**
 * Checks that the GTIN in a key credential begins with the GS1 Company
 * Prefix licensed to its issuer, and that each prefix licensed in the
 * recognition chain begins with the prefix licensed one level above it.
 *
 * @param {object} credential - The key credential being validated.
 * @param {object} options - The validation options.
 * @param {Array<object>} options.chain - The rest of the recognition
 *   chain; `chain[0]` recognizes the issuer of `credential`.
 * @param {string} options.action - The action being evaluated.
 * @param {string} options.evaluationDateTime - The evaluation time.
 *
 * @returns {{valid: boolean, errors: Array<object>,
 *   warnings: Array<object>}} The validation result.
 */
export default function validate(credential, options) {
  const errors = [];
  const warnings = [];
  const getId = value => typeof value === 'string' ? value : value?.id;

  // throw on any option this validator does not recognize
  const knownOptions = new Set(['chain', 'action', 'evaluationDateTime']);
  for(const name of Object.keys(options)) {
    if(!knownOptions.has(name)) {
      const error = new Error(`Unrecognized option "${name}".`);
      error.type =
        'https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-data-model#UNRECOGNIZED_OPTION_ERROR';
      throw error;
    }
  }

  // the prefixes are read from the recognition chain, so it is required
  const {chain} = options;
  if(!Array.isArray(chain)) {
    errors.push({
      type: 'https://id.gs1.org/validators#MISSING_CHAIN',
      title: 'Missing recognition chain',
      detail: 'This validator requires the recognition chain.'
    });
    return {valid: false, errors, warnings};
  }

  // `prefixes[i]` is the prefix that `chain[i]` licenses to the issuer of the
  // credential one level below it, which is `credential` when `i` is 0
  const prefixes = [];
  for(let i = 0; i < chain.length; ++i) {
    const issuerId = getId((i === 0 ? credential : chain[i - 1]).issuer);
    const entity = [].concat(chain[i].credentialSubject)
      .find(subject => subject.id === issuerId);
    if(typeof entity?.licenceValue !== 'string') {
      errors.push({
        type: 'https://id.gs1.org/validators#MISSING_LICENCE',
        title: 'Missing licence value',
        detail: `No licensed prefix found for ${issuerId}.`
      });
      return {valid: false, errors, warnings};
    }
    prefixes.push(entity.licenceValue);
  }

  // a GTIN-14 in a GS1 Digital Link URI: https://id.gs1.org/01/{gtin}
  const gtin = new URL(credential.credentialSubject.id).pathname.split('/')[2];
  if(!gtin?.slice(1).startsWith(prefixes[0])) {
    errors.push({
      type: 'https://id.gs1.org/validators#PREFIX_MISMATCH',
      title: 'Prefix mismatch',
      detail: `GTIN ${gtin} does not begin with prefix ${prefixes[0]}.`
    });
  }
  for(let i = 1; i < prefixes.length; ++i) {
    if(!prefixes[i - 1].startsWith(prefixes[i])) {
      errors.push({
        type: 'https://id.gs1.org/validators#PREFIX_MISMATCH',
        title: 'Prefix mismatch',
        detail: `Prefix ${prefixes[i - 1]} does not begin with prefix ${prefixes[i]}.`
      });
    }
  }

  const anchorId = getId(chain.at(-1).issuer);
  if(anchorId !== 'did:web:id.gs1.org') {
    warnings.push({
      type: 'https://id.gs1.org/validators#NOT_GS1_ANCHORED',
      title: 'Chain not anchored at GS1 Global Office',
      detail: `Chain ends at ${anchorId}, not at the GS1 Global Office.`
    });
  }

  return {valid: errors.length === 0, errors, warnings};
}

If Healthy Tots had issued a key credential for the GTIN `09507000134352`, and the chain ended at the GS1 Global Office, the [=validation function=] would return the following [=validation result=]. 

{
  "valid": false,
  "errors": [{
    "type": "https://id.gs1.org/validators#PREFIX_MISMATCH",
    "title": "Prefix mismatch",
    "detail": "GTIN 09507000134352 does not begin with prefix 9506000."
  }],
  "warnings": []
}

Errors

When an algorithm in this section fails, a [=verifier=] raises an error and describes it with a ProblemDetails object, as defined in the [[[VC-DATA-MODEL]]] specification. The `type` property identifies what went wrong. The types below are used by the algorithms in this section.

The first four types below are not yet defined in the [[[VC-DATA-MODEL]]] specification. This specification proposes that they be added to it, so that other specifications can use them as well.

https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-data-model#RETRIEVAL_ERROR
A resource could not be retrieved. This covers an identifier that does not resolve, a [=recognized entity credential=] that cannot be obtained, and a [=verifiable presentation=] that cannot be fetched from a service endpoint. The [=URL=] that was being retrieved SHOULD be provided in the `ProblemDetails` object.
https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-data-model#LIMIT_EXCEEDED_ERROR
A configured limit was exceeded. This covers the time a [=verifier=] allots to an operation and the maximum recognition chain depth it traverses. The limit that was exceeded SHOULD be identified in the `ProblemDetails` object. When a time limit runs out during a retrieval, the [=URL=] being retrieved SHOULD be provided as well.
https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-data-model#CREDENTIAL_VALIDATION_ERROR
A [=credential validator=] could not be evaluated. This covers a [=credential validator=] whose `type` or `mediaType` the [=verifier=] does not support, a [=verifier=] that cannot provide a sandbox for [=validator code=], [=validator code=] that fails to load, or that throws an exception or rejects without identifying a more specific error type, and a result that is not in the expected form.
https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-data-model#UNRECOGNIZED_OPTION_ERROR
A [=credential validator=] was given an option that it does not recognize. The name of the option SHOULD be provided in the `ProblemDetails` object.
https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-data-model#CRYPTOGRAPHIC_SECURITY_ERROR
A proof, or the digest of a resource, failed to verify. This type is already defined in the [[[VC-DATA-MODEL]]] specification.

UN GRID Cross-Border Trade Example

This entire example is currently under review by UNTP and might be changed based on input from that group and others.

The United Nations Global Registrar Information Directory (GRID) is a publicly accessible digital directory, hosted by the United Nations under the auspices of UN/CEFACT, that lists authoritative registrars, including national business registers, land registers, and trademark registers, from participating UN Member States. GRID addresses a critical verification gap in global trade and public administration: when supply chain participants, customs authorities, or trade finance lenders receive trade-relevant [=verifiable credentials=] from a foreign entity, they typically have no direct relationship with that entity and cannot independently verify its legitimate registration. Because billions of trade documents are exchanged globally every year, manual verification is not feasible. GRID solves this by providing a trustworthy, scalable map of the world's authoritative registrars, enabling automated verification and mitigating the risks of impersonation and fraud.

The resulting verification chain involves three tiers of credential, each issued at a different time, with a different lifecycle, and by a different issuer:

  • A trade document [=verifiable credential=], such as a commercial invoice, issued by the exporter's DID, with a lifecycle of weeks to a few months and issued in volumes of billions per year.
  • A `RecognizedEntityCredential` issued by the national registrar's DID to the individual exporter, attesting that the exporter's DID is controlled by the registered legal entity, with a lifecycle spanning years to decades and one credential per registered entity.
  • A `RecognizedEntityCredential` issued by GRID's DID to the national registrar, attesting that the registrar is a recognized, sovereign authority, with a lifecycle measured in decades. There are roughly one thousand such authoritative national registers worldwide.

The discovery process runs from the trade document toward the trust anchor using [=identifier-based discovery=] at each step, because publishing a single credential that lists all registered entities in a national register, with potentially two million entities updated hundreds of times per day, and tens of millions of verifiers retrieving the list multiple times per day, might be a scale that is impractical for the national register to achieve. Instead, each registered entity holds its own individual `RecognizedEntityCredential` and publishes it at a `WhoisService` endpoint in its DID document. The national registrar likewise publishes the `RecognizedEntityCredential` that GRID issued to it at its own `WhoisService` endpoint. This approach decentralizes the publication of the list such that each recognized entity in the ecosystem shares the burden of distributing the information. Holder-Based Delivery

The [=credential-based discovery=] and [=identifier-based discovery=] processes described in Section [[[#algorithms]]] both require a [=verifier=] to retrieve information from the network. Credential-based discovery fetches the [=recognized entity credential=] named by a `recognizedIn` property, and identifier-based discovery resolves the [=issuer=]'s identifier and fetches a [=verifiable presentation=] from a service endpoint. A [=verifier=] that is offline, or that is unwilling to contact a third party during a transaction, cannot perform either process.

In holder-based delivery, the [=holder=] supplies the [=recognized entity credential=] to the [=verifier=] alongside the [=verifiable credential=] it supports, using the "certificate stapling" mechanism described in Section [[[#recognizedentitycredential]]]. The [=holder=] provides every [=verifiable credential=] needed to establish the recognition chain, so the [=verifier=] evaluates the chain locally. Privacy for the [=holder=] improves as well, because no list publisher learns which [=issuer=] is being verified.

Holder-based delivery depends on each [=recognized entity=] holding a [=recognized entity credential=] of its own, rather than on a single [=verifiable credential=] that enumerates every entity in an ecosystem. A registration authority that issues one credential per registered entity, as in Section [[[#un-grid-cross-border-trade-example]]] above, lets that entity present its own recognition information without contacting the authority.

The three ways of delivering a [=recognized entity credential=] to a [=verifier=] disclose different amounts of information about the verification itself. Holder-based delivery discloses the least, because the [=verifier=] contacts no third party and no other party learns that the verification occurred. Retrieval from a list publisher discloses more, because that publisher learns that a verification is taking place or might soon, although a [=verifier=] that retrieves an entire [=recognized entity credential=] rather than a per-entity resource does not reveal which [=issuer=] it is checking. Retrieval from a `WhoisService` endpoint, by a [=verifier=], as described in Section [[[#identifier-based-discovery]]], discloses the most, because the endpoint belongs to the [=issuer=] being verified and that [=issuer=] therefore learns of every verification of its own [=verifiable credentials=]. Threat T4: Privacy Leakage via List Fetch describes this exposure and the measures that reduce it.

Because neither party can retrieve anything during the exchange, both cache what they need in advance. The [=holder=] caches:

  • The [=verifiable credentials=] it intends to present.
  • The [=recognized entity credential=] issued to it by the authority that recognizes it, and any further [=recognized entity credentials=] needed to reach a widely recognized trust anchor, as described in Section [[[#credential-based-discovery]]].

The [=verifier=] caches:

  • The identifiers of the [=issuers=] it accepts as trust anchors, along with the [=controlled identifier documents=] and verification key material needed to verify proofs from those [=issuers=] without resolving their identifiers.
  • Any files referenced by the `credentialValidator` property of a [=recognized action=] it intends to enforce, since those cannot be fetched while offline.
  • Revocation or status information, which is no fresher than the moment the [=verifier=] went offline. See Section [[[#stale-credentials]]] below.

The [=verifier=] evaluates the presentation without any network access. It verifies the proof on the [=verifiable presentation=], confirms that the `challenge` and `domain` match the ones it issued, and verifies the proof on each contained [=verifiable credential=] using its cached key material. It then performs [=recognition validation=], as described in Section [[[#recognition-validation]]], on the chain consisting of the food safety certificate followed by the [=recognized entity credential=]. That process confirms that the `issuer` of the certificate, `did:web:processor.utopia.example`, appears as the `id` of a [=recognized entity=] in the `credentialSubject` of the [=recognized entity credential=], that the facility is recognized to `issue` credentials conforming to the schema named by that [=recognized action=]'s `credentialValidator`, and that the chain terminates at `did:web:food-safety.utopia.example`, an [=issuer=] that the [=verifier=] has configured as an anchor.

Because [=recognition validation=] is independent of how the [=verifiable credentials=] were obtained, the [=verifier=] applies exactly the same process here as it would to a chain it had discovered for itself. Holder-based delivery changes only the discovery step.

Stale Credentials

A [=verifier=] is not obliged to accept a [=holder=]-provided [=recognized entity credential=]. As noted in Section [[[#recognizedentitycredential]]], a [=verifier=] that requires more recent information might ignore the provided [=verifiable credential=] and retrieve the [=recognized entity credential=] itself once it regains connectivity. An offline [=verifier=] cannot do so during the exchange, and therefore accepts a bounded staleness risk. Recognition might have been revoked after the [=holder=] cached its [=recognized entity credential=], or after the [=verifier=] last refreshed its own cached data.

Deployments reduce that risk by configuring a maximum acceptable age for stapled [=recognized entity credentials=], by issuing [=recognized entity credentials=] with validity periods appropriate to the expected offline interval, and by refreshing cached trust anchor and status information whenever connectivity is available. Related guidance is provided by threat T10: Stale Recognition Acceptance.

Every check the [=verifier=] performs in this example is cryptographic and local. Holder-based delivery changes how the [=recognized entity credential=] reaches the [=verifier=], not the checks that the [=verifier=] performs on it. A [=holder=] cannot alter a [=recognized entity credential=] without invalidating its proof, although a [=holder=] does choose which [=verifiable credentials=] to present; see threat T8: Unvetted Recognized Entity Credential Propagation.

Relationship to Other Technologies

This specification was designed with awareness of existing "trust infrastructure" technologies, three of which are described below. The data model defined in this specification is capable of expressing many of the concepts found in these technologies and is designed to interoperate with them via the `recognizedIn` property.

ETSI Trust Service Lists

The European Telecommunications Standards Institute (ETSI) defines a format for Trust Service Lists (TSLs) in [[?ETSI-TRUST-LISTS]], which is used across the European Union to publish lists of trusted service providers — entities authorized to issue electronic signatures, seals, timestamps, and other trust services under the eIDAS regulation. Each EU member state publishes a national TSL, and the European Commission publishes a List of the Lists (LoTL) that references each national TSL, forming a well-defined, hierarchical trust federation.

ETSI Trust Service Lists share several conceptual goals with this specification: both describe entities that are recognized to perform specific actions, associate those entities with identifying metadata, and are intended to be consumed by relying parties during validation. However, this specification differs from ETSI Trust Service Lists in several key ways:

  • Format: ETSI Trust Service Lists are expressed as signed XML documents. This specification uses JSON-LD and the Verifiable Credentials Data Model, enabling integration with the broader decentralized identity ecosystem.
  • Governance: ETSI Trust Service Lists are governed by a centralized, hierarchical structure rooted in EU member state authorities. This specification supports both centralized and decentralized governance models, allowing any entity to act as a recognizing authority within its own ecosystem.
  • Scope: ETSI Trust Service Lists are scoped to electronic trust services regulated under eIDAS. This specification is domain-agnostic and can express recognition across any sector, including education, healthcare, and financial services.
  • Holder-centric sharing: ETSI Trust Service Lists are published at well-known URLs and fetched by relying parties. This specification additionally supports "certificate stapling", where a holder presents a recognized entity credential directly to a verifier, reducing the need for verifiers to contact a central registry during each interaction.

X.509 Certificate Authority Lists

X.509 [[RFC5280]] is a standard for public key infrastructure (PKI) that defines the format for certificates and certificate chains. Certificate Authorities (CAs) are entities trusted to issue X.509 certificates that bind a public key to an identity. Trust in a CA is typically established through inclusion in a browser or operating system trust store — a curated list of root CAs — or through a certificate chain that traces back to such a root.

X.509 CA infrastructure and this specification both address the problem of establishing whether a given entity can be relied upon to perform a specific action within a given context. However, this specification differs from X.509 CA lists in several key ways:

  • Data model: X.509 certificates encode identifier and public key association in a binary ASN.1 format with a fixed schema. This specification uses a flexible, extensible JSON-LD data model that can express arbitrary metadata about recognized identifiers and the actions that the entities they identify are authorized to perform.
  • Trust hierarchy: X.509 PKI relies on a strictly hierarchical chain of trust rooted in a small number of globally trusted root CAs. This specification supports a web-of-trust model where multiple, overlapping, and competing recognition registries can coexist, and ecosystem participants can choose which recognizing authorities they trust.
  • Action semantics: X.509 certificates express key usage and extended key usage flags to constrain what a certificate may be used for, but these are limited to a fixed set of cryptographic operations. This specification expresses recognition for arbitrary, domain-specific actions such as issuing a particular type of credential, enabling richer and more context-specific claims of recognition.
  • Decentralized issuance: Adding a new CA to existing trust stores requires acceptance by browser vendors and/or OS maintainers, which is a centralized and often slow process. In contrast, this specification allows any entity to issue a `RecognizedEntityCredential` and to share it within its ecosystem without requiring approval from a central gatekeeper.

ISO/IEC 18013-5 mDL VICAL

ISO/IEC 18013-5 defines the mobile Driving License (mDL), a standard for expressing driver's licenses as mobile documents (mDocs). As part of that ecosystem, the standard defines a Verified Issuer Certificate Authority List (VICAL), which is a signed data structure containing a list of entities that are recognized as certificate authorities empowered to issue mDLs. A relying party uses the VICAL to determine whether the issuer of a presented mDL is trusted within its jurisdiction.

The VICAL concept and this specification share the goal of enabling relying parties to determine whether a credential issuer is recognized within a particular ecosystem. However, this specification differs from the mDL VICAL in several key ways:

  • Format: The VICAL is a CBOR-encoded, COSE-signed data structure which is tightly coupled to the mDL ecosystem. This specification uses JSON-LD and the Verifiable Credentials Data Model, making it suitable for a broader range of credential types and deployment environments.
  • Scope: The VICAL is specific to the mDL use case and the ISO/IEC 18013-5 trust model. This specification can use any type of credential to express recognition of any type of verifiable credential and any kind of action; it is not limited to issuing credentials for driving licenses.
  • Action expressiveness: The VICAL records CAs which are trusted to issue mDL certificates, but does not have a general mechanism by which to express the scope or other types of actions a CA is recognized to perform. This specification's `RecognizedAction` structure explicitly captures the actions a recognized entity is authorized to perform and against which credential schemas, enabling more fine-grained authorization.
  • Decentralization: The VICAL is managed by a designated VICAL provider within each mDL ecosystem and distributed through centralized channels. This specification allows any entity to publish a recognition credential without relying on a designated central provider, supporting more open and composable trust ecosystems.
